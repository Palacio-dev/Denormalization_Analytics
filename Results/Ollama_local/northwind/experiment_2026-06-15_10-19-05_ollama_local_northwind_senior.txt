# Principal Data Architect Report: Analytical Model Transformation

## Phase 1 – Schema Assessment

### Analysis of Source OLTP Schema
The provided schema is the classic **Northwind** dataset, a normalized relational model optimized for Transaction Processing (OLTP). Key characteristics include:
1.  **Granularity**: The `orders` and `order_details` tables represent transactional granularity. Aggregating this directly for reporting requires significant join operations.
2.  **Join Complexity**: Analytical queries typically require joining `customers` $\to$ `employees` $\to$ `orders` $\to$ `order_details` $\to$ `products` $\to$ `categories` $\to$ `regions` $\to$ `territories`.
3.  **Reference Data**: Tables like `categories`, `customers_demographics`, `regions`, and `territories` are small but frequently joined.
4.  **Data Types**: The use of `varchar` for IDs (e.g., `customer_id` as `varchar(5)`) is a legacy artifact of the original application and will be converted to integer surrogate keys for analytical performance.
5.  **Hierarchy**: The `employees` table has a self-referencing hierarchy (`reports_to`), which is complex to traverse in a star schema without flattening or recursive CTEs.

### Entity Relationships & Cardinalities
*   **1:N**: Customer $\to$ Orders; Product $\to$ Order Details; Employee $\to$ Orders (many-to-many via territories or direct assignment).
*   **N:M**: Employee $\leftrightarrow$ Territory (handled via `employee_territories` junction table).
*   **N:M**: Product $\leftrightarrow$ Category (handled via `products` junction logic, though often stored as a direct foreign key in Northwind).

### Query Access Patterns
*   **Sales Analysis**: Sum of sales by region, country, and employee.
*   **Product Performance**: Sales per category, top products, stock levels.
*   **Customer Segmentation**: Sales by customer type, demographics, and geography.
*   **Supply Chain**: Sales by shipper, supplier, and product status.

## Phase 2 - Analytical Modeling Strategy

### Chosen Architecture: **Star Schema with Dimension Flattening**
We will adopt a **Star Schema** because it offers the highest query performance for read-heavy workloads. However, given the depth of the hierarchy (Region $\to$ Territory $\to$ Employee $\to$ Orders) and the small size of dimension tables, we will employ **Dimension Flattening** (absorbing dimensions into fact tables) for the most common aggregation paths.

**Justification:**
1.  **Join Reduction**: The most critical analytical query (Sales by Region/Country/Employee) currently requires 5+ joins. In a flattened star schema, this becomes a single table scan or 1-2 joins.
2.  **BI Compatibility**: BI tools (Tableau, PowerBI, Looker) perform poorly with deep star schemas requiring multiple joins. A "wide" fact table is preferred.
3.  **Read Optimization**: Analytical queries are read-only. The trade-off of increased storage due to redundancy is negligible compared to the gain in query speed and code simplicity.
4.  **Surrogate Keys**: We will replace `varchar` IDs with `bigint` surrogate keys to enable efficient indexing and hashing.

### Denormalization Decisions & Trade-offs

| Decision ID | Description | Performance Objective | Join Reduction | Redundancy | Maintenance Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **D1** | **Flatten Geography**: Merge `customers`, `employees`, `orders`, and `order_details` into a unified `sales_fact` table. Include `region`, `country`, `city`, `postal_code`, `state` (from `us_states`), and `territory` columns directly. | Eliminate joins between geography tables and facts. Enables instant filtering by region/country. | Reduces 4-5 joins to 0-1. | ~20-30% storage increase due to repeated city/region codes. | Requires data pipeline to keep geography columns updated when source data changes. |
| **D2** | **Flatten Hierarchy**: Embed `territory_id`, `territory_description`, `region_id`, `region_description` into `sales_fact`. | Allows direct grouping by Territory or Region without joining `territories` or `regions` tables. | Eliminates join to `territories` and `regions`. | Moderate redundancy for territory/region strings. | High; territory descriptions change rarely, but updates must propagate to the fact table. |
| **D3** | **Flatten Product/Cat Info**: Embed `product_name`, `category_name`, `supplier_name`, `quantity_per_unit`, `discontinued` status into `sales_fact`. | Enables "Product Sales" reports without joining `products` or `categories`. | Eliminates joins to `products` and `categories`. | High; product names/categories are static but duplicated per order. | Minimal impact on writes; data freshness is key. |
| **D4** | **Absorb Demographics**: Add `customer_type_id`, `customer_desc` directly to `sales_fact`. | Enables immediate segmentation analysis. | Eliminates join to `customer_demographics`. | Low (small text fields). | Negligible. |
| **D5** | **Create Surrogate Keys**: Replace `varchar` IDs with `BIGINT` (e.g., `customer_id` becomes `customer_skey`). | Enables efficient B-tree indexing and hash joins. | N/A (Optimization at storage level). | N/A | Requires ETL to generate keys on load. |

*Correction Note in DDL*: The standard Northwind schema does not have `product_id` in the `orders` table. The `orders` table links to `customers` and `employees`. The `order_details` table links `order_id` and `product_id`. The `products` table links to `suppliers` and `categories`. The `orders` table does *not* have a direct link to `shipper` other than via `ship_via` (which references `shipper_id`). The previous DDL for `sales_fact` assumed a direct link for `ship_via` which is correct, but the `sales_hierarchy_view` logic needs to strictly follow the Northwind relationships.
