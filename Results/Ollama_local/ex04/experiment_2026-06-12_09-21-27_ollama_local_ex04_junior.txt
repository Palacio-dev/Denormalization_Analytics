### Analysis and Denormalization Strategy

As a Data Engineer, my goal is to optimize this schema for **analytics and reporting** (OLAP workloads) rather than transactional integrity (OLTP). In analytical contexts, frequent joins between small, granular tables (like `demography`, `employment`, `education`) on the same `year` and `country_code` can be expensive.

#### 1. Relational Schema Analysis
*   **Fact Table:** `survey` acts as the central "Fact" table containing the primary metrics (welfare, poverty, Gini) indexed by `id_survey`.
*   **Dimension/Context Tables:**
    *   `country` and `region`: Hierarchical context.
    *   `demography`, `employment`, `education`, `economy`, `health`: These are highly granular, year-specific tables that share the same foreign key structure (`country_code`, `year`, `id_...`).
    *   `decile`, `population`, `life_expectancy`: Supporting details often linked via `id_survey` or `id_demography`.
*   **Relationships:** The current schema requires multiple joins to reconstruct a complete profile for a specific country-year combination (e.g., joining `survey`, `demography`, `employment`, `economy`, `health` to get a full picture).

#### 2. Denormalization Strategy
To reduce joins, I will adopt a **Star Schema** approach where the `survey` table becomes the central hub, and all year-specific attributes are denormalized directly into it.

**Key Decisions:**
1.  **Merge Year-Specific Tables:** Instead of keeping `demography`, `employment`, `education`, `economy`, and `health` as separate tables, I will flatten their relevant columns into the `survey` table. Since `survey` already has `survey_year`, this allows grouping by year without joining.
2.  **Remove Redundant Dimensions:** The `region` table can be kept as a small lookup table, but `country` details (name, region_code) will be flattened into `survey`. The `decile` table is often part of the survey data itself; if it's not a separate entity, it can be flattened.
3.  **Handle Many-to-Many/One-to-Many:**
    *   `population` and `life_expectancy` are linked to `demography` (which links to `country`). To avoid complex joins, I will assume that for a given `survey_year` and `country_code`, the population and life expectancy data are representative or aggregated. If granular age/gender data is strictly required, it might need a separate "lookup" table, but for high-level reporting, flattening the `value` columns into `survey` is usually sufficient.
    *   `health` is already linked to `country` and `year`.
4.  **Primary Key Change:** The `survey` table will remain the single source of truth with `id_survey` as the PK. All other tables will be dropped or merged.

**Trade-offs:**
*   **Write Performance:** Inserting data will become significantly slower because every new record in `demography` or `employment` will require an insert/update into the massive `survey` table.
*   **Data Integrity:** We lose the ability to update a specific year's employment data without touching the survey record (though this is acceptable for read-heavy analytics).
*   **Storage:** Slightly increased storage due to redundancy, but negligible compared to the I/O savings from eliminating joins.
