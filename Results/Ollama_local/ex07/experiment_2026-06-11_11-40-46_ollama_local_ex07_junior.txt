### Explanation of Changes

1.  **Table Merging**: The original schema had a normalized structure with separate tables for Owners (`Proprietarios`), Rivers (`Rios`), Locations (`Localizacoes`), Municipalities (`Municipios`), and States (`Estados`). The denormalized approach merges these into a single `Usina` table.
2.  **Attribute Migration**:
    *   `proprietario` (VARCHAR) is added to `Usina` to replace the join with `Proprietarios`.
    *   `rio` (VARCHAR) is added to `Usina` to replace the join with `Rios`.
    *   `lat`, `long` (DOUBLE PRECISION) are added to `Usina` to replace the join with `Localizacoes`.
    *   `municipio` (VARCHAR) and `uf` (CHAR(2)) are added to `Usina` to replace the join with `Municipios` (and `Estados` since `uf` links them).
3.  **Foreign Key Removal**: All `FOREIGN KEY` constraints referencing the other tables are removed because those tables are no longer needed as separate entities in this analytics context.
4.  **Query Performance**: Queries that previously required `SELECT ... FROM Usina JOIN Proprietarios ... JOIN Rios ... JOIN Localizacoes ... JOIN Municipios ...` can now be executed as `SELECT ... FROM Usina`, drastically reducing CPU usage and network latency.
5.  **Data Redundancy**: While this increases storage size slightly (due to repeating `uf`, `municipio`, etc.), it eliminates the overhead of maintaining complex foreign key relationships and improves read performance significantly, which is the primary goal for analytics workloads.
