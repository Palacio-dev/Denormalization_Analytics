### Analysis & Strategy

**1. Schema Analysis:**
The provided schema is a classic "Northwind" retail database structure. It features:
*   **Core Entities:** Customers, Employees, Suppliers, Products, Territories, Regions.
*   **Transactions:** Orders and Order Details.
*   **Relationships:** Many-to-Many (Customer-Employee via Territories, Employee-Employee via Reports), One-to-Many (Customer-Orders, Product-OrderDetails).
*   **Redundancy:** Address data is duplicated in `customers` and `employees`. `ship_via` references `shippers` but `ship_name` is duplicated in `orders`.

**2. Inferred Workload & Bottlenecks:**
In a typical Northwind scenario, the heaviest read workloads involve:
*   **Customer Reporting:** Joining `customers` with `orders` and `order_details` to calculate sales per customer.
*   **Product Analytics:** Joining `products` with `categories`, `suppliers`, and `order_details` to find stock levels, sales volume, and supplier performance.
*   **Territory Management:** Joining `employees` with `territories` and `orders` to analyze sales by salesperson and region.
*   **Bottleneck:** The current schema requires multiple JOINs (e.g., `customers` -> `orders` -> `order_details` -> `products` -> `suppliers` -> `categories`). As data grows, these joins become expensive due to sorting and hashing on foreign keys.

**3. Denormalization Strategy (One-Big-Table):**
To achieve the "One-Big-Table" model:
*   **Flattening:** All columns from every table will be moved into a single table named `northwind_flat`.
*   **Primary Key:** A composite Primary Key will be generated based on the unique business entities: `(customer_id, order_id, product_id, employee_id, supplier_id, territory_id, region_id)`. This ensures uniqueness across the entire dataset.
*   **Foreign Keys:** Removed entirely. Instead of `FK`, we rely on the Primary Key structure and data integrity checks at the application level or via unique constraints on specific combinations if needed.
*   **Derived Attributes:** No new columns are added; existing columns are simply relocated.
*   **Prefixing:** To avoid ambiguity (e.g., `customer_id` exists in `customers`, `customer_customer_demo`, `orders`, etc.), I will strictly use the **original column names** as they are unique within the source tables, but since the target is one table, I must ensure the composite PK covers all entities. *Correction*: In the Northwind schema, `customer_id` is unique in `customers` and `orders`. `product_id` is unique in `products` and `order_details`. `employee_id` is unique in `employees` and `orders`. `supplier_id` is unique in `suppliers` and `products`. `territory_id` is unique in `territories` and `employee_territories`. `region_id` is unique in `region` and `territories`.
    *   *Constraint Handling*: Since we cannot have duplicate column names in a single table, and the original schema uses the same ID names for different entities, I will **prefix the column names** with the Entity Name (e.g., `cust_customer_id`, `ord_order_id`, `prd_product_id`, `emp_employee_id`, `sup_supplier_id`, `terr_territory_id`, `reg_region_id`) to ensure strict adherence to "Each column name must appear EXACTLY ONCE per table" while maintaining semantic clarity.
