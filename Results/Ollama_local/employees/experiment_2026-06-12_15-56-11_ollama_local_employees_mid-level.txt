### Analysis and Redesign Strategy

#### Section 1: Analysis and Structural Decisions

**Summary:**
The original schema follows a strict Third Normal Form (3NF) structure typical of transactional OLTP systems. It separates entities (Employee, Department) from relationships (Department_Employee, Department_Manager) and attributes (Salary, Title). For read-heavy analytical workloads (OLAP), this results in excessive JOINs, complex date-range logic across multiple tables, and potential data duplication during reporting. The strategy here is to adopt a **Star Schema** approach, consolidating related attributes into dimension tables and aggregating measures into fact tables where appropriate, while heavily denormalizing the dimension tables to include all necessary attributes for common analytical queries.

**Structural Decisions:**
1.  **Consolidate Department Dimensions**: Merged `department` and the `department_id` columns from `department_employee` and `department_manager` into a single `dim_department` table. This eliminates the need to join three separate tables to get department details.
2.  **Consolidate Employee Dimensions**: Merged `employee` and `employee_id` columns from `department_employee`, `department_manager`, `salary`, and `title` into a single `dim_employee` table. This is critical for reporting on "Who is in which department?" without joining back to the main employee table.
3.  **Create Aggregated Fact Tables**:
    *   Created `fact_employee_dept_history` to track the overlap of employees and departments over time.
    *   Created `fact_employee_salary` to track salary changes.
    *   Created `fact_employee_title` to track title changes.
4.  **Flatten Attribute Logic**: Removed `from_date`/`to_date` columns from the dimension tables (`dim_employee`, `dim_department`) where they are static or rarely queried in isolation, pushing the date logic to the Fact tables.

**Attribute-Level Changes:**
1.  **Added Derived Attributes**: Added `is_manager` flag to `dim_employee` by checking if the employee exists in the `fact_employee_dept_history` table (indicating a manager role).
2.  **Added Current Status**: Added `current_department_id` and `current_title` to `dim_employee` by joining with Fact tables where `to_date` is NULL (indicating active status).
3.  **Removed Redundant Columns**: Removed `gender` from the Fact tables as it is only relevant at the employee level.

**Trade-offs:**
1.  **Data Freshness vs. Query Speed**: The fact tables (`fact_employee_dept_history`, etc.) act as snapshots. If the source data updates frequently, these fact tables must be rebuilt or updated. This introduces a latency between data ingestion and availability in the analytical model, but it drastically speeds up query execution.
2.  **Storage Overhead**: The denormalized dimensions store data that technically belongs to the employee (e.g., manager status), increasing storage usage slightly, but the reduction in JOINs outweighs this cost for analytical queries.
3.  **Complexity in Updates**: Changing an employee's manager relationship requires updating the Fact table and potentially re-calculating the `is_manager` flag in the dimension, which is more complex than a simple foreign key update in the normalized schema.

#### Section 2: Query Patterns

**Query Patterns:**
1.  **Pattern: "List all employees with their current department and title"**
    *   *Motivation*: A common dashboard requirement to see the organizational structure.
    *   *Benefit*: In the original schema, this requires joining `employee`, `department_employee`, `department`, and `title` (handling NULLs for non-managers or non-titled employees). In the new schema, it requires only a simple JOIN between `dim_employee` and `dim_department` (and potentially `dim_title`), as the "current" status is pre-calculated in the dimensions.
2.  **Pattern: "Show the history of department changes for a specific employee"**
    *   *Motivation*: HR reporting on career paths.
    *   *Benefit*: Direct aggregation on `fact_employee_dept_history` allows for time-series analysis without complex date-range logic across multiple normalized tables.
3.  **Pattern: "Calculate average salary per department over time"**
    *   *Motivation*: Budget analysis.
    *   *Benefit*: The `fact_employee_salary` table allows grouping by `department_id` and `date` (or year) efficiently, avoiding the need to join back to the `employee` table to get department info.
