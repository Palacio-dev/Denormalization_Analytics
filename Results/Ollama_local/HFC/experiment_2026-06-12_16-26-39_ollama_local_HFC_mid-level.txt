### Analysis and Redesign Strategy

The provided schema represents a classic "Transaction-Oriented Normalized" design typical of relational databases (like PostgreSQL) used for CRUD operations. It relies heavily on foreign keys to maintain referential integrity and reduces data redundancy to ensure ACID compliance. However, for an analytical environment (BI dashboards, reporting), this structure suffers from:
1.  **High Join Complexity:** Queries to calculate statistics (e.g., "Total specimens per family per year") require joining 5-7 tables, leading to performance bottlenecks on large datasets.
2.  **Fragmented Dimensions:** Information about a specimen is scattered across `tombos`, `especies`, `familias`, `generos`, `solos`, `relevos`, etc.
3.  **Lack of Aggregability:** Aggregating measures (counts, sums, averages) requires complex subqueries or window functions to reconstruct dimensions.

To optimize for read-heavy workloads, we will adopt a **Star Schema** approach with significant denormalization of dimensions. We will create a central `fact_specimen` table and a set of denormalized dimension tables that include necessary attributes from multiple normalized tables.
